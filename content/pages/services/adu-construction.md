## What Counts as an ADU

"Accessory dwelling unit" is the planning department's name for a second, self-contained living space on a single-family lot. Everything Arizonans actually call these projects falls under it:

- **Casitas and guest houses** — detached structures, usually in the backyard, with their own entrance, bathroom, and typically a kitchenette or full kitchen.
- **Garage conversions** — an existing attached or detached garage rebuilt as finished living space.
- **In-law suites** — attached additions or converted interior space arranged so a parent or adult child lives connected to the household but behind their own door.

The label matters less than the function: a space someone can genuinely live in, built legally, permitted properly, and tied into utilities correctly. That last part is where an experienced general contractor earns their keep.

## Arizona's ADU Law, in Plain Terms

Arizona changed the rules in homeowners' favor. A statewide law — HB 2720 — now requires many of the state's larger cities to allow ADUs on lots zoned for single-family homes, and the cities it covers include most of the East Valley and Phoenix-metro communities we work in every week. In practice, that means the question for most of our clients is no longer *whether* they're allowed to build, but *what* makes sense on their particular lot.

We've kept the statute detail out of this page on purpose, because the specifics — how many units a lot can hold, size allowances, setback and parking rules, what cities can and can't require, and how rentals are treated — deserve a careful, sourced explanation. That's exactly what our **[Arizona ADU law guide](/guides/arizona-adu-law)** provides, city by city. Read it before your consultation if you like homework; skip it if you'd rather we walk you through what applies to your address in person. Either way, two things always remain lot-specific: your parcel's dimensions and utilities, and your HOA's covenants. We review both at the free consultation, before you've spent a dollar.

## Casitas and Guest Houses, Built From the Ground Up

A detached casita is a complete small building: foundation, framing, roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, insulation, drywall, and finishes. We build it the same way we build a [custom home](/services/custom-homes), because structurally that's what it is — just scaled down. Our crew handles the site work and slab, frames and dries in the structure, runs the systems, and finishes the interior to match the quality of your main house, from flooring to fixtures.

Ground-up construction gives you the most freedom: you choose the footprint, the layout, the ceiling height, where the windows face, and whether the unit gets a full kitchen or a kitchenette. It's the right path when privacy matters — for a long-term tenant, or for family members who want genuinely separate households sharing one property.

## Garage Conversions: The Faster, Leaner Path

If your garage sits full of boxes instead of cars, you may already own the shell of your ADU. A conversion starts with the slab, walls, and roof in place, which typically makes it the quickest and most budget-conscious way to add living space. Our scope on a conversion usually includes insulating walls and ceiling to livable standards, replacing the garage door with a framed, finished wall and proper entry, extending electrical and HVAC, adding plumbing where the layout calls for a bathroom or kitchenette, and finishing the interior so that nothing about the room says "this used to be a garage."

Conversions still require permits — this is a change of use, and the city will inspect it like any other habitable space. We coordinate that permitting just as we do on ground-up builds, so the finished room is legal, insurable, and counted when it matters.

## In-Law Suites and Multigenerational Living

A large share of the ADU work we do isn't about rent at all. It's about an aging parent who shouldn't be across town, or an adult child saving for their own place, or simply a household that works better with one more private door in it. Attached in-law suites — built as an addition or converted from existing space — keep family close while preserving the privacy both sides need: a separate entrance, a private bathroom, and ideally a small kitchen setup so nobody is negotiating over one coffee maker.

If you're weighing an attached suite against simply enlarging the house, our [room additions](/services/room-additions) page covers that comparison — the short version is that an ADU is a self-contained dwelling, while an addition grows the main home. We build both, so our advice at the consultation isn't steering you toward anything; it's matching the structure to how your family actually plans to use it.

## The Rental-Income Question

Long-term rental is one of the most common reasons Valley homeowners build an ADU, and it's a legitimate one: a well-built casita is housing someone will pay for, on land you already own. We won't quote you rental figures — that's your local market and your decision, not a construction company's promise — but we will build the unit so it works as a rental: durable finishes, sensible utility arrangements, sound separation from the main house, and a layout a tenant actually wants to live in. The rental rules under Arizona's ADU law are covered properly in [the guide](/guides/arizona-adu-law).

## What Shapes the Cost of an ADU

Every lot is different, so we price from a site visit rather than a brochure. The variables that move the number most are the ones worth knowing up front: ground-up construction versus conversion of existing structure; the unit's size and whether it includes a full kitchen; how far electrical, water, and sewer have to travel from your existing service to the new space, and whether your panel has capacity to spare; site access for equipment and materials; and finish level. None of these are reasons to wait — they're the agenda for the free consultation, and you'll see each one priced plainly in the written quote.
