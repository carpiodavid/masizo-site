A kitchen remodel is really four or five projects wearing one name. New cabinets are carpentry. The sink you want to move is plumbing. The range, wall oven, and under-cabinet lighting are electrical. The permit for all of it is paperwork with the city. Hire a company that only does one of those, and you become the general contractor of your own kitchen — scheduling trades, refereeing gaps, and owning every problem that falls between them.

Masizo Development & Restorations remodels kitchens across Gilbert, Mesa, and the Phoenix Valley as a licensed Arizona general contractor ([[LICENSE_GC]]): cabinets, countertops, islands, backsplash, flooring, plumbing, electrical, and permits, from one company on one schedule. ## One licensed company for everything the kitchen touches

The kitchen is the most trade-dense room in the house. Nearly any meaningful change crosses a licensing line: relocate the sink to an island and you are re-running supply and drain lines; replace a 1990s range with a modern one and you may need a dedicated circuit the old panel never carried; add pendants and under-cabinet lighting and you are in the wiring again.

Our contracting license covers that whole span, which changes how the project runs day to day. The cabinet schedule and the plumbing schedule belong to the same calendar. The electrician roughing in your island outlets knows the cabinet dimensions because it is one plan. And when the city inspector comes, one company is responsible for everything being right.

## Cabinets, countertops, and islands

Cabinets set the tone of a kitchen and most of its budget, so this is where we spend the most design time with you. The questions that matter are working questions: where do you actually cook, what do you store, what runs out of room today? From there we plan the cabinet layout — full-height pantry walls, deep drawer stacks instead of blind base cabinets, lighted glass uppers if you want display space — and pair it with countertop material suited to how you cook. Quartz earns its popularity in hard-working kitchens for stain resistance and low upkeep; granite brings natural variation and takes heat well; warm wood cabinet species pair beautifully with stone when you want the kitchen to feel less showroom and more home. We help you choose pairings in the showroom with slab and door samples, not from a website photo.

An island is the single most-requested feature we build, and it is as much infrastructure as furniture: seating overhangs that need the right support, a prep sink or outlets that need lines run under the slab, and clearances around it so two people can pass while the dishwasher is open. We design islands to fit the room rather than squeezing the room around an island.

Demolition and haul-off of the old kitchen — cabinets, counters, flooring, the dated soffit above the uppers — is part of the job.

## Appliances, electrical, and lighting

Modern appliances are hungrier than the circuits most Valley kitchens were built with. Induction ranges, double wall ovens, and high-CFM vent hoods frequently call for new dedicated circuits, and sometimes for panel work — exactly the discovery that stalls a cabinet-only remodeler and simply becomes a line item on ours. We rough in the electrical to match the actual appliance spec sheet, install the appliances, and vent hoods properly to the exterior.

Lighting is the cheapest transformation in the room: layered recessed lighting for general use, pendants over the island, and under-cabinet strips that put light on the counter where you are actually cutting — instead of your own shadow.

## Layouts, wall openings, and flooring

When the layout itself fights the cook — the refrigerator door blocking the walkway, a peninsula fencing the kitchen off from the family room — the fix is spatial. We open or remove walls (with engineered support and a permit when the wall is load-bearing), rework the kitchen triangle, and add the island the new space allows. Whole-house layout projects live on our [home remodeling page](/services/home-remodeling); if the kitchen needs more room than the house has, that is a [room addition](/services/room-additions) conversation.

New flooring ties the finished kitchen together, and we will talk honestly about sequencing: whether flooring runs under the cabinets or meets them, and how to match or transition to adjacent rooms so the new kitchen does not end at an awkward seam.

## The leak you cannot see yet

Pull the base cabinets out of an older kitchen and a familiar story appears more often than anyone would like: a slow supply or drain leak that has been quietly soaking the cabinet floor, the drywall, sometimes the slab-edge framing. The worst response is the common one — note it, shrug, and set the gleaming new cabinets right on top of it.

We are an IICRC-certified restoration contractor ([[LICENSE_RESTORATION]]) as well as a builder, so hidden moisture is handled properly and in-stream: we show you what we found, price the repair in writing, dry and replace the damaged material, fix the source, and then continue the remodel. Your new kitchen goes in over a sound, dry structure — which is the whole point of doing it once.

## Selections, costs, and the parts that drive both

After the free estimate, the selections phase is where the kitchen becomes yours: cabinet line and finish, counter material, backsplash tile, hardware, fixtures, lighting. We guide the choices, then handle ordering and delivery logistics — and we schedule demolition only after the long-lead items are confirmed, because a kitchen torn out while cabinets are weeks away is how horror stories happen.

What actually drives kitchen remodel cost in the East Valley? In rough order: cabinet scope (refacing vs. full replacement vs. custom), whether plumbing or walls move, countertop material and slab count, appliance tier, and electrical scope. We price all of it in your written estimate — and anything added mid-project is priced in writing before the work is done, never after. That rule holds whether the addition is your idea or the house's.

A word about surviving the remodel itself: you will be without your kitchen for the duration, so we help you plan for it. A temporary station — refrigerator, microwave, coffee maker, and a folding table in the dining room or garage — plus paper plates and a utility-sink dishwashing routine keeps a household functional. We tell you the realistic dark-weeks window up front so you can plan around it instead of discovering it.
