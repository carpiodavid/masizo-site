An Arizona roof lives a harder life than almost any other roof in the country. Ten months of punishing UV bake the flexibility out of shingles and underlayment, then monsoon season arrives with wind-driven rain, microbursts, and dust storms that find every weakness the sun created. ## Start with a real inspection, not a guess

A leak stain on your ceiling tells you water got in — it does not tell you where. Water travels along rafters, underlayment, and drywall before it shows itself, so the entry point is often several feet from the stain. That is why we inspect the entire roof, not just the area above the drip: field tiles or shingles, underlayment condition, flashing at every penetration (vents, skylights, satellite mounts), valleys where water concentrates, and the parapets and drains on flat sections.

You get the findings in writing: what we found, what it needs, and what it costs. If the roof needs a $400 repair, that is what the paper says. If it is at the end of its life, we will show you why — photos included — so the replace decision is made on evidence, not pressure.

## Repair or replace: how we call it

Repair is the right answer more often than roofing ads suggest. A slipped tile, a failed pipe-jack boot, cracked flashing, or a localized underlayment tear can be fixed for a small fraction of replacement cost, and when the rest of the roof is healthy, that is what we recommend.

Replacement earns its cost in a different situation: when the underlayment or shingle field is failing broadly, every storm opens a new leak, and each repair buys months instead of years. At that point patching is the expensive option — you pay repair prices repeatedly while interior damage accumulates between visits. Our rule is simple: we tell you which situation you are in, and we put the reasoning in the written estimate so you can get a second opinion on the same facts.

## Roof systems we work on

**Tile roofs.** Here is the thing most Valley homeowners are never told: concrete and clay tile is not the waterproof layer. Tile is armor for the underlayment beneath it — and that underlayment, not the tile, is what dries out and fails after a couple of decades of attic heat. A tile roof can look perfect from the street and leak like a basket. Our tile work includes lifting and relaying tile to replace failed underlayment, repairing broken or slipped tiles, and rebedding ridge and hip lines.

**Shingle roofs.** Asphalt shingles in Arizona fail from the sun as much as from storms — granule loss, curling, and brittle edges that monsoon winds then peel. We repair wind damage and flashing failures, and replace shingle roofs with materials rated for high-heat performance.

**Flat and low-slope roofs.** Common on Valley additions, patios, and territorial-style homes, flat roofs depend on their coating and on drainage. Ponding water, cracked coatings, and failed scuppers are the usual suspects; we repair, re-coat, and rebuild these sections so water leaves the roof instead of finding a way through it.

## Monsoon season and storm damage

From roughly June through September, the Valley's storms arrive violently: straight-line winds, horizontal rain, and haboobs that drive dust under flashing. The best money you can spend is before the season — a pre-monsoon inspection catches the slipped tile or cracked boot while it is a repair instead of a flooded hallway. After a storm, walk your yard and look for tile fragments or shingle pieces; if you find any, or if a stain appears on a ceiling, get the roof inspected before the next storm widens the opening.

Between seasons, two habits protect a Valley roof cheaply. First, keep drainage clear: leaves and dust dams in valleys, gutters, and flat-roof scuppers make water pond exactly where roofs are weakest. Second, look up occasionally — from the ground, with binoculars if you have them. Slipped or cracked tiles, lifted shingle edges, and rust streaks below flashing are all visible from the driveway, and every one of them is cheaper the month you spot it than the season after.

If a storm has already put water in your house, do not wait on the roofing schedule — our [24/7 restoration team](/services/restoration) handles the emergency dry-out, and the roof repair follows as part of the same project.

## Roof insurance claims, documented properly

Storm damage to a roof may be covered by your homeowner's policy, and the difference between a smooth claim and a frustrating one is usually documentation. Our process: we inspect and photograph the damage, separate storm damage from pre-existing wear (insurers will, so we do it first), help you file, and meet your adjuster on the roof to walk the evidence together.

What we will not do is promise that insurance will pay — no contractor controls that decision, and you should be wary of any roofer who sells a free roof before an adjuster has seen it. What we can do that most roofers cannot: when the claim includes interior water damage, our restoration license ([[LICENSE_RESTORATION]]) and IICRC certification let us repair the ceilings, walls, insulation, and flooring under the same claim and the same contractor. One company, one scope, one responsible party — instead of a roofer, a mitigation company, and a drywall sub all pointing at each other.
